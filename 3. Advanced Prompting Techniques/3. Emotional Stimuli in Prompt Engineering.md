### Emotional Stimuli in Prompt Engineering — Explanation & Summary

**Emotional stimuli prompting** is a technique where you add emotionally charged phrases (like urgency, importance, or pressure) to a prompt in order to influence how the model responds.

Examples include phrases like:

* “This is very important to my career”
* “You’d better be sure”
* “This is critical—do not get it wrong”

These phrases are not about changing the task itself, but about trying to influence the model’s *attention behavior*.

---

### Key Research Findings

Research into emotional prompting revealed several surprising results:

* The model actually pays **very little attention to the emotional phrases themselves**
* However, adding emotional language causes the model to pay **more attention to the important parts of the prompt**
* Key task words like *“positive,” “negative,” and “review”* receive stronger attention when emotional stimuli are present

---

### Example Prompt (as used in research)

```text prompt id="em1"
Determine whether a movie review is positive or negative.
```

---

### What the Research Discovered

* Words like **“positive,” “negative,” and “review”** received the highest attention from the model
* Emotional phrases did NOT directly influence those words
* Instead, emotional language indirectly improved performance by shifting overall attention toward important tokens

---

### Does Aggressive Language Help or Hurt?

Surprisingly:

* Even strong or aggressive phrasing like *“you’d better be sure”* did **not reduce performance**
* In many cases, it slightly improved attention to key parts of the prompt

---

### Why This Might Work (Theory)

The idea is:

* LLMs rely on **attention mechanisms**
* Emotional language may “heighten focus,” causing the model to allocate more attention to relevant tokens in the task
* This improved attention distribution can lead to **better predictions and accuracy**

---

### One-Sentence Summary

**Emotional stimuli prompting uses emotionally charged language to indirectly improve model performance by increasing attention to important parts of the prompt, even though the emotional words themselves are mostly ignored.**
